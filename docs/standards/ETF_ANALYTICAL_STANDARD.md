# ETF Analytical Standard

## Certified ETF Pillars

Every certified ETF must retain evidence for:

1. Return and trend
2. Risk and drawdown
3. Valuation and underlying fundamentals
4. Income and distributions
5. Fund structure and implementation quality
6. Diversification, concentration, and overlap
7. Regime and portfolio fit

## Evidence Requirements

Each pillar must preserve raw evidence, normalized and component scores, source lineage, observation dates, data-quality status, confidence, and explanations. Missing evidence must reduce confidence and must not be silently imputed or rewarded.

## Factor Core

The common equity ETF factor core covers valuation, quality, momentum, income, defensive characteristics, and growth. Strategy-aware extensions may apply to broad-market, dividend, growth, international, small-cap, or other approved mandates, but common evidence must remain visible for comparison.

## Returns and Benchmarks

Total return is the primary performance measure. Raw prices, distributions, splits, and material corporate actions must be preserved. Provider-adjusted evidence and internally reconstructed total returns must be reconciled under governed tolerances. Each ETF must be evaluated against its strategy benchmark, a common equity benchmark, and a cash hurdle.

## Risk

Risk assessment must include realized variability, drawdowns, tail risk, historical stress, hypothetical scenarios, and portfolio-level risk contribution. No single volatility or Value-at-Risk statistic may govern a certified decision.

## Forecasts

Forecasts must use governed, horizon-specific ensembles for 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years. The 3-year horizon is primary for strategic ETF decisions. Forecasts must publish ranges, probabilities, model dispersion, confidence, drivers, and lineage.

## Recommendations

Certified recommendations require eligibility gates, separate asset-attractiveness and portfolio-fit evidence, risk and confidence controls, stable action bands, and explanations. Adjacent rank changes alone must not force action changes.

## Validation

Historical validation must be point-in-time and walk-forward, prevent look-ahead and survivorship bias, compare against reasonable simple baselines, and report regime, cost, robustness, and practical-materiality results.
